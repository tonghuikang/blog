We now [^millennium] can see that it is a matter of time before AI can do any work that humans can do on a computer.

[^millennium]: This is written shortly after AI solved some Millenium Problems.
    I have been confident that AI will improve, but I am still surprised that it has improved this quickly.

It is also a matter of time before AI can do any work that humans can do.

There are three things I worry about currently.

I do hope to figure this out, at least for myself.



## Financial security

In 10 years, what will we still pay humans to do?

I find it really hard to think of examples that will stand the test of time.

Here are some examples that I could think of that might still be true in 10 years

- You still probably cannot sue model providers for the faults in arbitrary model outputs.
    There might still be humans who are responsible for understanding work done by superintelligent AI, and making decisions on how to allocate resources.
- Airplanes already have autopilot.
    The pilot might just need to press a few buttons, to fly a plane from one location to another.
    However, we still need two pilots to fly every plane, and seem to continue requiring this.

Given that you might not earn an income in 10 years, there might be some things that you want give up.

This may including buying a house [^housing-prices], starting a family, or traveling the world.

[^housing-prices]: I am not sure if the world has priced in the potential drop in housing prices.



## Sense of purpose

You may already have something like one million dollars, which is probably enough to live comfortably.

It is possible that you can retire somewhere low-cost and play (AI-generated) video games for the rest of your life.
Then, what is the purpose in life?

Do you really need to continue to create a positive impact to the world? [^purpose]

[^purpose]: If you have done something that you are proud of, I imagine that you could be less worried about your legacy.



## Existential risks

Money and the sense of purpose does not matter if the humanity ends.

This is what the labs worry about. [^disconnect]

[^disconnect]: One thing I had to realize is that anxiety that I experience might not fully overlap with the lab employees.
    I do strongly agree someone should think really hard on this problem similar to how we think about terrorism.
    However, I do not have the means to influence the trajectory, I currently do not work for a lab.
    


The most conceviable way to do this is that someone could engineer a virus that wipes out humanity.

Covid-19 killed 7 million people.
If there is a virus more infectious as Covid-19, with a longer incubation period, and a higher motality rate, it could have killed a lot more people.

This is similar to the threat of terrorism. [^surveillance]

[^surveillance]: I do not think of the threat of terrorism justifies mass surveillance.
    There are some things that should be controlled, however.
    Typical people should not be given access to advanced equipment that could create viruses, for example.



## Conclusion

The world will continue to spin.

While anything now feels possible, there are some things we know is impossible.
We still live under the same laws of Physics.

I do hope to figure this out, at least for myself.
